# How to brief AI like a new starter

## Why answers come back average

Business owners can explain their business problems all day long to a person. Then they open Claude and type one sentence. The AI fills every gap with the most average guess, and the answer comes back average.

The fix is not a cleverer prompt. It's more context. Explain it like you're talking to someone who just started in your team.

## The three parts

**1. What the business does and who the customer is.**
A new starter can't do good work without knowing who it's for. Say what you sell, who buys it, what they care about and how you talk to them. You only need to say this once if you save it.

**2. The job you want done, and exactly how it looks.**
Describe the job the way you'd hand it to a new starter on their first day. Then describe what done looks like: the format, the length, where it goes, who reads it. If you have a good example, give it. One example beats a paragraph of description.

**3. What's gone wrong before, and how to avoid it next time.**
This is the part everyone skips and it's the part that saves the most time. If the last version was too long, too salesy, missed a detail or went to the wrong person, say so. Add anything that must never happen without your say-so.

## Why talk instead of type

When you type, you write the short version. When you talk, you say the long version without noticing: the tangents, the examples, the "oh and one more thing". That's the context the AI was missing. There's no limit on how long you can talk, so give it as much as you can.

Any dictation works. Wispr Flow is what we use: you hold a key, speak like a human, let go, and it types what you said wherever your cursor is, including straight into Claude or Codex. Setup is in [wispr-flow.md](wispr-flow.md).

## What a good brief has (the checklist)

- [ ] Who the business is and who the customer is
- [ ] The job, in new-starter detail
- [ ] What done looks like (format, length, where it goes, who reads it)
- [ ] An example of a good one, if the job has a look or a voice
- [ ] What went wrong last time and what to do instead
- [ ] What must never happen without asking

If a brief is missing a box that would change the result, ask for it. If it wouldn't, pick a sensible default and say so.

## Quick examples of the difference

| Typed one-liner | The new-starter version (said out loud) |
|---|---|
| "Write a follow-up email to a client." | "We quoted a bathroom reno for a couple in Burleigh last Tuesday, about forty grand. They liked the design but went quiet. Write a short follow-up from me, friendly, not pushy, and offer a fifteen-minute call. Last time I sent a long one and they never replied." |
| "Make a job ad." | "We need a second admin person, three days a week, mostly answering the phone and booking jobs in. Our last hire left because the ad made it sound like a sales job. Keep it plain, list the actual days and the software we use." |

Both are made up, to show the level of detail.
