# The new-starter brief: copy-paste template

Fill this in, or say it out loud and let Claude fill it in for you. Paste it at the top of any chat with Claude, ChatGPT, Codex or whatever you use. Delete the hints in brackets.

```text
Here's the brief. Treat me like your boss and you like someone who just started in my team.

1. THE BUSINESS AND THE CUSTOMER
What we do: [one or two sentences, the way you'd say it at a barbecue]
Who the customer is: [who they are, what they care about, what they hate]
How we sound: [plain, friendly, formal, Aussie, no jargon...]

2. THE JOB AND HOW IT LOOKS WHEN IT'S DONE
The job: [what you want done, in as much detail as you'd give a new starter]
Done looks like: [the format, the length, where it goes, who reads it]
A good example: [paste one, or describe the best one you've seen]
What I'll give you: [notes, a transcript, a spreadsheet, last month's version...]

3. WHAT'S GONE WRONG BEFORE AND HOW TO AVOID IT
Last time it went wrong because: [what happened]
So this time: [what to do instead]
Never: [anything you must not do without asking me: send, spend, delete, publish, name a customer]

Before you start, ask me up to three questions if anything here would change the result. Otherwise, go.
```

## A filled-in example (a made-up business, to show the level of detail)

```text
1. THE BUSINESS AND THE CUSTOMER
What we do: We're a plumbing business on the Gold Coast. Mostly hot water systems and blocked drains for homes.
Who the customer is: Homeowners who've got a problem right now and want someone today. They hate waiting and hate surprise prices.
How we sound: Friendly, straight to the point, no plumber jargon.

2. THE JOB AND HOW IT LOOKS WHEN IT'S DONE
The job: Write the text message we send after every job asking for a Google review.
Done looks like: Under 300 characters, one link, sounds like it came from the plumber who did the job.
A good example: "Thanks for having us out today, Sarah. If we did a good job, a quick Google review helps us heaps: [link]"
What I'll give you: The customer's first name and the job type.

3. WHAT'S GONE WRONG BEFORE AND HOW TO AVOID IT
Last time it went wrong because: The old message was long and read like a marketing email, so nobody clicked.
So this time: Short, personal, one ask.
Never: Offer a discount for a review.
```
